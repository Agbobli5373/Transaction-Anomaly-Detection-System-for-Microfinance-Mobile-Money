# The High threshold rises with the market's spend

The Alert budget is a number of Alerts per day, but the High threshold is a fixed Risk score, set on quiet days (60–90). On a festive day the whole market is busier and spends more, every amount-driven score rises with it, and the fixed threshold raises twice the Alerts the budget allows, on the days Analysts are busiest: 213–269 a day over Easter (days 92–95) against a budget of 100, and 67–160 on other days (#47). The festive season is a Benign anomaly, so the extra Alerts are almost all legitimate Transactions.

So the High threshold now rises with a **market spend index**: the market's spend in the last 6 and the last 24 complete hours, each over the same hours on each of the previous 7 days, whichever ratio is larger (1 is a normal day). On a day with index `i`, High is set as if the Alert budget were `i ^ 1.5` times smaller (never more than 3 times), from the score distribution of the threshold days: the bundle stores the High threshold at each divisor from 1 to 3 in steps of 0.05 and interpolates between them. The Medium threshold does not move, so a Transaction that stops being High on a busy day lands on the Medium watch list, where Analysts can still see it. A Critical rule hit is High on any day. The elasticity (1.5) and the limit (3) are `surge_elasticity` and `surge_max_divisor` in `config/evaluation.toml`; 0 keeps High fixed, and a bundle trained before this change loads and behaves as before.

The index is a feature of the shared feature function (`market_spend_index`), computed from history strictly before the Transaction: it reads the market's spend in each complete clock hour before the Transaction's own hour, from the offline replay, from Postgres, and through the Redis cache (which delegates it to Postgres). It is not a model input; the models are unchanged. Postgres keeps the hourly sums in `market_hours`, maintained by a trigger on `transactions`, because summing eight days of Transactions on every assessment would be far too slow. Many workers store Transactions at once, so each hour is spread over 16 rows to keep them from queuing on one.

## Considered Options

- **A cap on each day's High Alerts, counted as they are raised.** It guarantees the number, but the day's later Alerts are the ones lost, whatever their score. On the evaluation days a cap of 140 cost 3 points of recall overall and 9 on Social-engineering push, because fraud does not wait for the morning. Ranking each day's Alerts afterwards would cost nothing, which shows the surplus is the lowest-scoring Alerts, but that is hindsight: the scoring path cannot know the day's later scores. It would also make a Risk band depend on the order Transactions arrive in and on other Transactions' Alerts, which breaks retries returning the stored result and replay matching live.
- **Deflate the amount-driven features by the market's amount level.** Aimed at the cause, but it only removes part of it: with every amount-driven feature deflated by the market's amount level (tried on the saved bundle, without retraining), festive days still averaged 171–181 High Alerts, because the market is also 1.4 times busier, and a busier market raises 1.4 times the Alerts at a fixed threshold whatever the scores. It also changes what every feature means and needs the models retrained.
- **Features for busy hours that the models learn to discount.** The only festive season the models train on is New Year (days 0–1), two days with hardly any fraud, so they have nothing to learn it from.

## Consequences

- The Alert budget now holds on festive days, and High needs no one to notice that a season has started. Within a festive day the High Alerts that remain are those with the highest scores, so recall falls only a little (see `docs/evaluation/stacked.md`).
- A real surge in fraud that also raises the market's spend would raise the threshold too. The limit of 3 bounds that, Critical rule hits are exempt, and what falls to Medium is still on the watch list.
- The elasticity was chosen on the evaluation days, the only ones with a festive season outside training. It should be revisited if the data holds a second.
- Without a week of history the index is empty and High does not move.
- Every assessment reads the market's hourly spend from Postgres, even when the Redis cache answers the rest: about 3,000 small rows, summed by the database. It is the same for every Transaction of an hour, so a per-process cache by hour is the first thing to add if it shows up in the load test.
